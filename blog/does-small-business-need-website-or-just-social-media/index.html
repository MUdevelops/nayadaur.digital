Does Your Small Business Need a Website or Just Social Media?

By NayaDaur Digital · 20 October 2026

Many owners ask this: "My Facebook page gets messages. Do I really need a website?" The honest answer is that it depends on how your customers find you and what they need before buying.

Here is a practical way to decide, without pressure either way.

What social media does well

  • Lets customers see your latest products and offers
  • Makes it easy to message you
  • Builds familiarity through photos and short videos
  • Costs little to start

What social media does poorly

  • Information is hard to find once posts scroll away
  • You do not control the platform. Reach, rules and accounts can change or be restricted
  • Google does not treat it like an organised source of answers about your business
  • Prices, services and policies end up scattered across posts and chats

What a website adds

A website is a place you own. It can show your services in order, your prices or price ranges, your location and your contact buttons. It can appear when someone searches Google for what you sell, even if they have never heard of your page. It also makes a business look established to customers who want to check you before paying.

A quick decision guide

These are examples of how the choice often falls, not rules:

  • A street food stall or very small shop with mostly walk-in customers: start with a Google Maps profile and WhatsApp Business. A website can wait.
  • A boutique that sells through Instagram: keep Instagram, and add a simple catalog website once you have many products and repeat questions.
  • A clinic, academy, workshop or any service people research before choosing: a website and a Google profile are worth having early.
  • A manufacturer, supplier or company serving other businesses: a website is usually expected.

The sensible middle path

You do not have to choose one. A common low-cost order is: Google Business Profile first, WhatsApp Business second, then a simple one-page website that links everything together. Add more pages when you have the content and the enquiries to justify them.

Questions to ask yourself

  1. Do customers search Google for what I sell?
  2. Do I answer the same questions repeatedly by message?
  3. Would I lose trust if a customer could not find me online?
  4. Do I want to be less dependent on one platform?

If you answer yes to two or more, a website is probably worth building.

Questions about this guide

Is a Facebook page enough?

For some very small, walk-in businesses it may be enough to begin with. If customers search Google or want to compare, a website helps.

Are free website builders okay?

They can work for a basic start, but check the domain, ads, design limits and whether you can move your content later.
